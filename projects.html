01 · Amazon Academy

Amazon Academy’s Daily Digest

Turning learner state into the next best action.

Context

I led the product, learning and behavioural design of Daily Digest, evolving a static “Question of the Day” into a personalised daily entry point for IIT-JEE and NEET students.

Amazon Academy already had videos, practice, chapter tests, mock tests and analytics. The problem was helping students decide what to do next.

Core idea
01Learner state
02High-impact action
03Short completable session
04Route back into product
Learner state

I mapped engagement, performance, preparation stage and exam proximity into intervention flows: reinforce progress, recommend remediation, surface practice, or sometimes do nothing.

We didn’t wait for perfect ML. I translated the logic into rules and cohort-level interventions that could ship immediately, while leaving a path toward individualised recommendations.
Adaptive routing

A response stopped being just right or wrong and became evidence for what should happen next.

SignalResponse
InterpretGap / misconception / strength
ActionPractice / remediation / deeper work
UpdateNew learner evidence
Content & assessment

I defined the taxonomy across tips, worked examples, puzzles, concept refreshers and distractor-based diagnostics. Each diagnostic distractor mapped to a misconception, so a wrong answer carried more signal than a score alone.

My role
OwnedLearner-state framework, intervention logic, content taxonomy, notification logic and experience specification.
Worked acrossProduct, UX, Data Science, Engineering, QA, SMEs and external vendors.
Result
+40%week-8 retention

Alongside gains in daily active users, practice attempts, chapter-assessment completion and paid conversions.

02 · Sri Chaitanya · BYJU’S · Amazon

Building knowledge graphs for personalized learning

Giving learning products a structured model of what is being learned.

Why

A content library knows what assets it contains. A learning system also needs to know what those assets teach, how concepts depend on one another, and where a learner can go next.

Foundational work

I began with knowledge-graph work for Grades 8–10 at Sri Chaitanya. At BYJU’S, while overseeing the K–12 video vertical, I carried that work forward and helped build a broader K–12 graph from scratch.

The graph mapped concepts, sub-concepts and prerequisites, helping SMEs chunk curriculum into teachable units and link video, practice and assessment to the same model.

03 · Amazon Academy

Building diagnostic systems & learning paths

Turning assessment into a decision point inside the learning journey.

Problem

Students were dropping off between video learning and practice. Moving straight from a lesson into a full practice set was too large a leap for some learners, while the same path was shown to students with different levels of readiness.

Core model
01Learn
02Diagnose
03Interpret
04Route
Diagnostic layer

I mapped where diagnosis belonged: before practice, during revision, between activities or between videos — and how much assessment could be introduced without breaking the journey.

The goal was not just a score, but deciding whether a learner should continue, practise, revise or remediate.

Assessment quality

I worked with SMEs to validate diagnostic content, introduced randomised QA, and experimented with game-based formats to make diagnostics easier to start.

My role
OwnedDiagnostic framework, placement logic, learner flows, assessment structure and remediation routing.
Worked withSMEs, Product, UX and Engineering.
04 · Amazon Academy

Teacher Readiness Dashboard

Turning learning intelligence into better teaching decisions.

Problem

A lot of what the platform knew about student difficulty stayed inside the system. Teachers preparing a lecture still relied heavily on experience and instinct.

Knowledge underneath

The harder part was structuring the knowledge behind the dashboard. I worked with SMEs and senior faculty to capture tacit expertise into reusable libraries: misconceptions, difficult concepts, common errors, exam-critical ideas and shortcut methods.

Closing the loop

I designed the system so teaching could become another source of learning. If a difficulty repeatedly surfaced with one cohort, it could become context for the next teacher preparing the same topic.

05 · Schools & classroom programs

Taking learning beyond the screen

Building schools, flipped classrooms and experiential science programs.

Building a K–12 school

I built a K–12 school in my family’s village in Andhra Pradesh, designing the curriculum, teaching approach and operating model before handing it over to my father to lead.

It now serves around 500 students.

Curriculum · teaching practice · operations · community

Flipped classrooms

At BYJU’S and in schools in Bangalore, students worked through video lessons before class, leaving classroom time for discussion, problem-solving and practice.

The classroom became a feedback loop into the product: where students hesitated, what they misunderstood, what they skipped, and what still needed a teacher.

Classroom observation · product feedback · instructional design

Experiential science programs

I built and ran science programs where students performed the experiments themselves rather than only watching demonstrations.

The model was later adopted across other BYJU’S-affiliated schools.

Experiential learning · science instruction · program design
